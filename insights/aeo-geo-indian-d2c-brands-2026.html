Why Indian D2C brands are missing from AI answers (and how to fix it in 2026)

31 Oct 2026·10 min read

76% of Indian shoppers now use AI to research products, yet AI answers cite marketplaces far more than brand sites. A practical AEO and GEO playbook for D2C teams.

The short version
  • 76% of Indian shoppers now use AI to research products, but AI answers cite marketplaces far more than brand sites.
  • AEO, GEO and SEO are one job with three names — not three separate channels to run.
  • Seven specific moves shift AI citations; the biggest is being named by sources AI already trusts.
  • A 30-day checklist and a way to measure visibility without guessing.

Indian shoppers have moved faster to AI than almost any market on earth, and most D2C brands have not adjusted how they publish content. The result is a quiet gap: your ads work, your store converts, but when a customer asks an AI assistant which brand to buy, the answer rarely comes from your website.

The short answer

Answer engine optimisation (AEO) and generative engine optimisation (GEO) are the work of making your brand the thing an AI assistant reads, quotes and recommends. In India, that work is not about adding schema tags to a homepage. It is about publishing clear, answer-first pages, being present on the marketplaces and communities those engines actually read, and making sure every claim about your brand is consistent across the web.

What is actually happening in India

The adoption numbers are no longer early-adopter numbers. A McKinsey survey published in October 2026, covering around 21,000 consumers across 11 markets, found that 76 per cent of Indian consumers already use AI tools to search for products, compare options or make purchase decisions. Only China was ahead, at 80 per cent. The global average was 53 per cent. The same study found that 87 per cent of Indian AI users trust AI chat and search tools, against a global average of 73 per cent.

That trust shows up in purchase behaviour. A BCG study reported by The Economic Times in October 2026 put AI search influence at roughly a quarter of all purchase journeys in India, and in 65 per cent of those journeys shoppers said the AI answer influenced their final brand choice. LLMs are now among the top three sources of discovery for Indian consumers, alongside social media and traditional search.

YouGov's July 2026 study of Indian online searchers adds the habit layer: six in ten start at least one new query with an AI assistant every day, and 75 per cent say they use AI assistants to find information more than they did a year ago.

Where AI actually gets its answers about Indian brands

This is where most brand teams guess wrong. The common assumption is that AI reads your website and summarises it. In Indian D2C, that is usually not what happens.

The Cited Index, published in July 2026, tracked 226 Indian brands across ten categories through five engines and captured 760 citations. In its original reading, marketplaces and aggregators accounted for 33 to 57 per cent of citations in D2C categories, community and social sources for 19 to 29 per cent, and earned media for about 3 per cent. Worth knowing: the publishers revised that citation layer in August 2026, after finding their first method read publisher names out of answer prose instead of the URLs the engines actually cited. On the corrected data marketplaces carry a smaller share and brand sites become the largest single source. Treat the split as directionally useful, not settled.

Global studies point the same way. A large-scale analysis of more than 100,000 AI responses found that only about 2.9 per cent of citations point at the brand's own domain, while roughly three quarters point at third-party corporate and peer-brand pages. When engines do cite content, the ranked best-of listicle is the single most-cited format, at about 35.7 per cent of content citations.

The practical implication is uncomfortable but useful: if your product page is weak on the marketplace, and nobody is writing about you anywhere else, no amount of on-site optimisation will put your brand into an AI answer.

AEO, GEO and SEO are one job with three names

Google's 2026 guidance is blunt about this: optimising for generative AI search is still search engine optimisation, because AI features retrieve pages through the core ranking systems. A page has to be indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet before any AI feature can use it. Indexing is never guaranteed.

So treat the three terms as layers of one system:

  • SEO gets you eligible: crawlable, fast, indexed, with clean titles and internal links.
  • AEO gets you extractable: direct answers, question-shaped headings, short definitional paragraphs, FAQs.
  • GEO gets you quotable: statistics, named sources, original data, and consistent facts across third-party sites.

The seven moves that actually shift AI citations

1. Put the answer in the first two paragraphs

Engines extract the earliest clear statement on a page. If your page opens with brand poetry and reaches the point in paragraph nine, the extractable version of your answer will come from a competitor's page instead. Write the direct answer first, then the nuance.

2. Fix the marketplace before you fix the blog

For Indian D2C, the product detail page on the marketplace is often the most-cited page about you. Titles, bullet points, specifications, pricing, variant names and imagery all need to match your own site exactly. Inconsistent naming between Amazon and your store is one of the most common reasons a brand gets described incorrectly by an assistant.

3. Earn mentions where engines already read

Third-party coverage is what makes an answer name you. The same analysis found that when a cited third-party page names a vendor, the answer is far more likely to name that vendor too, and the effect is stronger for smaller brands. Prioritise the domains that engines already cite in your category, then earn coverage there with original data, expert commentary and honest reviews rather than press releases.

4. Build comparison and best-of pages

Because listicles dominate content citations, pages that compare options are disproportionately valuable. A well-built comparison page, whether on your site or on a review site, can be reused across dozens of different prompts.

5. Use statistics and quotes

A study of 10,000 real-world queries found that pages containing quotes and statistics had 30 to 40 per cent higher visibility in AI responses than pages without them. This is the cheapest content upgrade available: put numbers, dates and named sources into the pages you already have.

6. Make sure the content exists in HTML

AI crawlers are unreliable at executing JavaScript. If your product details, prices or article body only appear after a client-side render, there is a real chance the engine sees an empty page. Server-rendered HTML, a clean robots.txt, and a well-maintained llms.txt file all help. Structured data is useful for rich results, but research so far suggests markup alone does not create AI visibility, content quality does.

7. Be one consistent entity everywhere

Assistants build a picture of your brand from your site, your marketplace listings, your social profiles, reviews and press. Different founding years, different category descriptions and different product names across those surfaces produce a confused entity. Write one canonical description of the brand and use it everywhere, including on your About page and in organisation schema.

A 30-day checklist for a D2C brand

  • Week 1: Ask five AI assistants twenty real buying questions in your category. Record which brands are named and which domains are cited.
  • Week 1: Compare your marketplace product pages against your own PDPs for name, price, variants and claims. Fix the differences.
  • Week 2: Rewrite your top five money pages so the first 60 words answer the main question directly.
  • Week 2: Add statistics, dates and named sources to those pages. Remove unsourced superlatives.
  • Week 3: Publish one comparison page and one category explainer, both written to answer real prompts.
  • Week 3: Pitch two publications or communities that already appear in your citation list, with original data.
  • Week 4: Add FAQs with direct answers to your service and product pages.
  • Week 4: Re-run the same twenty prompts and compare. Change one variable at a time and re-audit every 14 to 30 days.

How to measure AI visibility

Traditional analytics will not show most of this, because a lot of AI discovery never produces a click. A third of Indian AI searchers say they most often click through to links supplied by the assistant, while 28 per cent usually stop after the answer. So track two layers:

  • Leading indicators: share of mentions against competitors for a fixed prompt set, citation share by domain, and whether the brand is described accurately.
  • Lagging indicators: branded search volume, direct traffic, and assisted conversions in your attribution model.

Google Search Console now includes a Generative AI performance report for supported Search features, and Bing Webmaster Tools reports citation activity across Microsoft's AI experiences. Neither covers every mention, so a manual prompt tracker maintained weekly is still the most reliable signal for a small brand.

Common mistakes

  • Optimising only the website while the marketplace listing carries the category.
  • Chasing schema markup as a shortcut instead of publishing extractable content.
  • Publishing opinion without numbers, dates or named sources.
  • Writing for keywords rather than for the questions people actually type into an assistant.
  • Treating AI visibility as a one-time project instead of a monthly loop.

FAQ

How long does GEO take to show results?

Brands that are already well known are recognised by engines within days, while new entities take longer. The practical loop is weeks, not quarters: make a change, then re-audit at 14 to 30 days.

Do I need to rewrite all my content?

No. Start with the pages that already rank and the pages that answer buying questions. Adding a direct answer at the top, real statistics and a short FAQ to existing pages is usually enough for the first cycle.

Is schema markup useless for AI search?

Not useless, but overrated as a shortcut. It helps eligibility and rich results. Research so far suggests that content quality additions, such as quotations, statistics and citations, produce the larger visibility gains.

Does AI search only favour big brands?

The early fear was that assistants would consolidate around a handful of names. The evidence is more mixed. BCG found that in more than half of AI-assisted journeys, consumers began considering brands they would not have found on their own. Small brands can compete by being readable, consistent and cited.

What should an Indian D2C brand do first?

Audit what the engines already say about you and your category, fix the marketplace and website mismatch, then publish answer-first pages with real numbers. That sequence fixes accuracy first and visibility second.

What we would do in the first month

Start with a citation audit of twenty prompts in your category, not with a content calendar. Fix the facts that are wrong or missing, because an assistant that describes you inaccurately will not recommend you confidently. Then earn two or three credible mentions on domains the engines already trust in your category, and rebuild your five highest-intent pages around direct answers.

AI visibility is not a channel you buy. It is a by-product of being clearly and consistently documented across the places machines read.
