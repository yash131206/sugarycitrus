Meta Ads for D2C brands.

Most Indian D2C accounts do not fail at targeting. They fail at creative volume, account structure and measurement. We fix those three, then scale what the data supports.

Who this is for

Brands spending roughly Rs 25,000 a month or more on Meta, with a product that already sells and someone who can ship creative changes quickly. If you are pre-launch or spending under Rs 10,000 a month on ads, a full retainer is usually the wrong shape — we will say so.

What the work includes

  • Account structure: consolidation where it helps learning, separation where it protects budget, and a naming system that makes reporting readable.
  • Creative testing: a brief and testing system that produces eight to fifteen new concepts a month, with a written read on which angle, hook and format is working.
  • Audience and signal: broad targeting where the creative is strong enough, clean conversion events, and a check on what the pixel is actually counting.
  • Measurement: blended CAC and contribution margin alongside platform ROAS, so a good month on Meta is not hiding a bad month overall.
  • Weekly readout: what changed, what it cost, what happens next. Written, not a dashboard link.

Where Meta Ads usually leaks money

  • Too few creatives. Five concepts a month cannot produce a readable signal. Fatigue arrives before the test finishes.
  • One campaign doing three jobs. Prospecting, retargeting and catalogue traffic in one ad set means none of them can be judged.
  • Platform ROAS taken at face value. If the pixel counts an add-to-cart that also happened last week, the number is flattering the channel.
  • Optimising for the wrong event. Purchases are sparse early on; a well-chosen upstream event often teaches the system faster without inflating reported revenue.
  • No exit rule. Spend continues because stopping feels like admitting failure. Every test should have a stop condition written before it starts.

How the month runs

  • Week 1: account and pixel audit, structure changes, first creative batch briefed.
  • Week 2: tests live, first read on cost per result and hook rate.
  • Week 3: scale the winners at a controlled rate, kill the losers, second batch briefed.
  • Week 4: written readout, next month's priorities, budget recommendation.

What it costs

Meta Ads management starts at Rs 8,000 and goes up to Rs 15,000 a month for a single-channel engagement, depending on spend and creative volume. Combined Meta and Google retainers sit higher. Ad spend and production are paid separately, directly by you.

Minimum engagement is three months, because that is the shortest period in which a test cycle produces a readable result.

Frequently asked questions

Is Meta still worth it in 2026?

For most Indian D2C categories, yes, but it works differently from 2021. Broad targeting, high creative volume and clean conversion data matter more than manual audience stacking.

How many creatives should we test per month?

Eight to fifteen new concepts is a realistic range for most D2C accounts. Fewer than five rarely produces a readable signal.

Do you produce the creative?

We build the strategy, briefs, scripts and iteration system. Production is handled by your team, by creators we coordinate, or through a production partner we scope with you.

What ROAS should we expect?

It depends on your margin, average order value and category. A brand at 65 percent gross margin can run a lower ROAS than one at 30 percent. We set the target from your unit economics in month one, not from an industry average.
